**Promotional text (≤170)**

Practise your varisais with a tanpura that listens. Free, offline, no ads, no account and no tracking.

**Description**

Purandara plays the graded Carnatic lessons on harmonium and tanpura, in tala, at your own Sa. Sing along and it marks every note you sing.

EVERY VARISAI, ALANKARAM AND GITAM, IN TALA
Graded pieces in Mayamalavagowla: the plain scale, then Sarali, Madhya Sthayi, Janta, Vakra Janta and Dhattu varisai, the seven alankarams (one in each suladi tala), the first gitams with their words, and swarajatis. The harmonium plays each swara at your Sa over a real tanpura, while the notation and the tala strip follow along. Slow it down to learn a line, take it up to fourth kala, count in before you start, or let it repeat.

SING ALONG, AND SEE EACH NOTE
Turn on the microphone and sing with the harmonium, or with just the tanpura and tala. Every note gets a mark: in tune, a little high or low, an octave off, or a different swara. After each pass you see how many notes landed and which ones need attention. Start gentle and make the marking stricter as your voice settles.

SETTLE ON SA BEFORE YOU BEGIN
Sing over the tanpura and watch your voice against the swara lines. Then hold Sa, Pa or upper Sa steady for 10, 20 or 30 seconds, and try to beat your best.

NOT SURE OF YOUR SA?
Sing your lowest and highest comfortable notes, and Purandara suggests a kattai that leaves room for lower Pa and upper Ri. Already have a Sa from your teacher? Pick it from the list, and nudge it a few cents to match their shruti box.

PICK UP WHERE YOU LEFT OFF
The home screen remembers the last exercise you practised and offers the next one in the teaching order.

MADE FOR PRACTICE, NOT FOR PROFIT
• Free: no price, no ads and no subscription.
• Offline: everything runs on your device. Airplane mode is fine.
• Private: no account and no tracking. Your voice never leaves the device.
• Accessible: works with VoiceOver and with large text.
• Open source under the MIT licence.

Named after Purandara Dasa, the Pitamaha of Carnatic music.
