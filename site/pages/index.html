Practise your varisais with a tanpura that listens

Purandara plays the graded Carnatic lessons on harmonium and tanpura, in tala, at your own Sa. Sing along and it marks every note you sing.

A practice room in your pocket

Every varisai, in tala

44 graded exercises in Mayamalavagowla: the plain scale, then Sarali, Madhya Sthayi, Janta, Vakra Janta and Dhattu varisai, then the seven alankarams, one in each suladi tala. The harmonium plays each swara at your Sa over a real tanpura, while the notation and the tala strip follow along.

Slow it down to learn a line, take it up to fourth kala, count in before you start, or let it repeat.

How to play a varisai

Sing along, and see each note

Turn on the microphone and sing with the harmonium, or with just the tanpura and tala. Every note gets a mark: in tune, a little high or low, an octave off, or a different swara. After each pass you see how many notes landed and which ones need attention.

Start gentle and make the marking stricter as your voice settles.

How sing-along marks your notes

Settle on Sa before you begin

Sing over the tanpura and watch your voice against the swara lines. Then hold Sa, Pa or upper Sa steady for 10, 20 or 30 seconds, and try to beat your best.

Warm up with Shruti practice

Not sure of your Sa?

Sing your lowest and highest comfortable notes, and Purandara suggests a kattai that leaves room for lower Pa and upper Ri. Already have a Sa from your teacher? Pick it from the list, and nudge it a few cents to match their shruti box.

Find your Sa

Pick up where you left off

The home screen remembers the last exercise you practised and offers the next one in the teaching order. Each set says what it trains, and each exercise shows its melodic shape, a tick once you have practised it, and your best sing-along result.
