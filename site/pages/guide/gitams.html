Gitams

Gitams are the first songs: a melody, a tala and words. Purandara has the four pillari gitams in raga Malahari that Purandara Dasa composed for beginners, then Analekara in raga Suddha Saveri.

Raga Malahari

Malahari is a janya of Mayamalavagowla. It climbs S R₁ M₁ P D₁ S′ and comes down S′ D₁ P M₁ G₃ R₁ S, and it has no Ni. The gitams also go a little below Sa (Padumanabha reaches lower Dha, written Ḍ) and above upper Sa (upper Ri, R′).

Analekara starts high, around upper Sa, and climbs to upper Ma. If your Sa is very high, the top notes are beyond the app’s harmonium: the player says so and offers the nearest Sa that fits.

Sections and words

  • Each part of the gitam starts with a heading: Charanam 1, Pallavi and so on. The refrain (the pallavi) is written out every time it is sung, so the notation always follows the singing.
  • The words are under the swaras, one syllable per beat. A dash means the syllable before it is held.
  • In Practice settings, Gitam notation chooses what a gitam shows: Swaras, Words or Both (the default). Learn the swaras first, then sing the words.
  • Screen readers read each avarta's swaras and then its words.

Singing along, and gamakas

The harmonium plays every note plain, but a gitam is sung with gamakas: some notes are shaken or slid into. Sing-along compares your voice with the plain note, so a gamaka can be marked high or low even when you sang it the way your teacher does. Set Strictness to Gentle for gitams, and learn the gamakas from your teacher.

Where the notation comes from

The swaras and words follow karnatik.com, checked note by note against Shivkumar Kalyanaraman’s archive. Sri Gananatha’s refrain follows the archive and the book Sangeeta Bala Padam, Part 1. Gitams are taught with small differences between traditions; if yours differs, sing it your teacher’s way. See Credits.
