Tuning: Just and Equal

The Tuning setting decides how the app pitches each swara above Sa. Just (tanpura) is the default; Equal (keyboard) is for practising alongside a keyboard or other fixed-pitch instrument.

A cent is a hundredth of a keyboard semitone. A difference of about 5 cents is hard to hear on a single note; 10 to 15 cents is clearly audible against a drone.

The two systems

Just intonation tunes each swara to a simple ratio with Sa: Pa is 3/2, Ma is 4/3, Antara Ga is 5/4, and so on. Notes tuned this way blend with the drone, without the slow wobble (beating) you hear when two notes are slightly apart; this is what a singer naturally does against a tanpura.

Equal temperament divides the octave into twelve identical semitones of 100 cents each. It is how keyboards are tuned, so that every key works the same way. Its fifths and fourths are very close to pure; its thirds and sixths are not.

The twelve swara positions

The app uses the sixteen Carnatic swara names over twelve positions. Four pairs share a position (R₂/G₁, R₃/G₂, D₂/N₁, D₃/N₂); the raga decides which name is used.

A positive difference means the Just note is higher than the keyboard note. “Keyboard note” is the Western name of the sounding pitch at the default Sa, C3; at another Sa everything moves with it.

Sa, Pa and Ma sound almost the same in both tunings. The largest differences are on the Ga, Dha and Ni positions, and on Ri for the three-semitone R₃.

The keyboard names are only for orientation: Carnatic G₃ at Sa C3 sounds the Western E, and M₂ is prati madhyama, not a Western major second.

Why Just steps are unequal

On a keyboard every semitone is 100 cents. In Just mode the steps between neighbouring positions vary, from 71 cents (R₃ to G₃) to 133 cents (D₂ to D₃). That is the price of pure intervals: a fixed set of twelve notes cannot have both equal steps and pure thirds and fifths. Equal temperament spreads the difference evenly, which is why its thirds and sixths are the notes that drift furthest from pure.

Shrutis, gamakas and the tanpura

Carnatic theory speaks of 22 shrutis; their number and exact values are debated, and the twelve swarasthanas are the working pitch framework. The app takes a deliberately simple position:

  • One ratio per position. These are the common just ratios; they are not a claim about all 22 shrutis. Other just tables choose slightly different ratios for some swaras (for example 64/45 for M₂ or 16/9 for N₂).
  • No gamakas. The reference is a plain harmonium note. Sing-along can mark a gamaka as high or low even when it was sung correctly, which is why Gentle strictness suits gitams and varnams.
  • The tanpura is a real recording (pa SA SA sa). It follows your Sa and fine tune, but it is not retuned when you switch to Equal: its Pa stays a natural fifth, only 2 cents from the keyboard’s fifth, so it works with either tuning.
  • Equal is there for practising with a keyboard, not because keyboard tuning is correct for Carnatic music.

What the setting changes

  • The pitch of the harmonium swaras in the player.
  • The pitch sing-along and Shruti practice expect from you. The in-tune width is set by Strictness, not by Tuning.

It does not change the tanpura, the notation, tala, tempo, kala or how strictly notes are marked. Changing it while playing restarts playback.

Choosing between them

Use Just (the default) when practising with the tanpura and your voice.

Use Equal when you practise with a keyboard, digital piano, tuner or other fixed-pitch instrument, so the app agrees with it note for note.

If you are unsure, use Just and fine-tune Sa to your teacher’s shruti box or recording. If your teacher’s reference is a keyboard, use Equal.
