Sing along

Turn on the microphone in the player and sing with the exercise. Purandara marks every note and sums up each pass.

Start singing

  1. Open an exercise and tap the microphone button. The first time, allow microphone access.
  2. Tap Play. Sing-along always starts with one cycle of count-in.
  3. Stay quiet until the harmonium starts. During the count-in the app listens to the room and to its own sound, so it can tell your voice apart from them.
  4. Sing with the harmonium. Keep each note plain and steady.

What the marks mean

MarkMeaning
✓In tune.
↑ ↓Close: a little high or a little low (orange).
↑ ↓High or low by more than close (red).
⇡ ⇣The right swara, but an octave high or low.
✕A different swara.
○Not heard: too little of your voice during the note.

Very short notes at fast speeds aren’t marked, but they still show in the pitch lane. Each note is judged on its steady middle, so a little scoop at the start doesn’t count against you.

The pitch lane and the reading

Above the notation, the expected notes of the current cycle are drawn as a staircase, with your voice traced over them, and lines for each swara. Below it, a reading names what you are singing, for example “P · in tune · +3¢ · G3”: the swara, how close it is, how many cents off, and the keyboard note. Screen readers hear the reading.

After each pass

A summary such as “Pass 1: 14 of 16 notes in tune or close”, and the notes that went wrong more than once, such as “Needs attention: R₁ high (2 of 2)”. Your best result for each exercise shows on the home screen; a pass only counts towards it if at least half its notes were heard.

Strictness

How close counts as in tune is a practice setting: Gentle, Steady or Precise. Start gentle while your voice settles, and tighten it as you improve.

Note separation and breathing

The Harmonium notes setting offers connected or lightly separated guide notes, without changing the exercise’s timing. A small gap makes successive notes easier to distinguish; it is not an instruction to breathe after every note. Holds remain sustained. Continuous accompaniment does not mean you must sing the entire exercise on one breath: take comfortable breaths at appropriate musical and word boundaries, with your teacher’s guidance.

Recovery and listen–repeat

Optional breaks between attempts are excluded from pitch scoring. The completed marks and summary stay visible while you rest; each new scored attempt receives a fresh count-in. Breathing or singing during a recovery break does not add marks. Stay quiet for the calibration count-in before your next turn.

In Listen, then sing, the model is not scored. Only your turn receives marks when Sing along is on. The model uses harmonium even when your response guide is off. Without Sing along, the response is an unscored practice interval and the app does not request microphone access.

On iOS, maintaining uninterrupted tanpura during scored practice can keep the microphone connection and its system indicator active during breaks and model playback. Those buffers are discarded before pitch analysis; break audio is not stored or sent. Stop ends the practice microphone session. On Android, capture stops for these intervals.

Harmonium guide off

In practice settings, set Harmonium guide to Off (drone and click) to sing each note on your own, with only the tanpura and tala to guide you. The marks still appear.
