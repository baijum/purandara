Practice settings

Open the settings with the sliders button in the player. The same controls exist on Android, iPhone and iPad, though the app groups them differently. Every change is saved on the device and applies to every exercise. Tempo and Speed are on the player itself, beside the notation.

Sa and fine tune

Sa is the home note: the harmonium, the tanpura and the marking of your notes all use it. Purandara offers G2 to C4; the default is C3 (1 kattai). The list is grouped by the voices that usually sing there, from lower voices to higher voices, and each choice shows its keyboard note and kattai, such as “D3 · 2 kattai”. You can also choose Sa from the home screen. More in Choose your Sa.

Fine tune moves Sa by up to 50 cents above or below the keyboard note, in one-cent steps; exact (0) is the default. A fine-tuned Sa is shown after the label, such as “C3 · 1 kattai +12¢”. It applies to the harmonium, the tanpura and the marking of your notes.

If a piece needs notes above the harmonium samples at the chosen Sa, the player says so and offers the nearest Sa that fits. You cannot start the piece until you choose a playable Sa.

On Android, Fine tune is directly below Sa; on iPhone and iPad it is at the top of the Sa list.

Tempo and Speed

Tempo is the speed of the tala beat: 30 to 120 beats per minute, in steps of 5, default 60. It is not the speed of the written notes.

Speed is the kala: 1 to 4, default 1. Each step doubles the number of swaras to each tala beat, while the beat keeps its tempo. A varisai written one swara to a beat has one per beat in first kala, two in second, four in third and eight in fourth. A piece written with several swaras to a beat starts from there: a swarajati written four to a beat has eight in second kala. A comma in the notation counts as a swara here, since it holds the one before it. The notation is regrouped to match: each block stays one tala cycle, with the swaras of each beat together under its mark (see At a faster speed).

At a faster speed a short piece may be played more than once to fill whole tala cycles; the line under the notation then says “Pass 2 of 4”. A varnam section already marked at double speed plays twice as fast again.

Fourth speed remains available as an optional challenge, not a requirement for every exercise. For the sources’ practice targets, how tempo and speed combine, and a way to build up gradually, see The speeds.

Sound and accompaniment

Tuning
Just (tanpura) is the default: each swara takes its natural ratio to Sa, the way a tanpura is tuned. Equal (keyboard) divides the octave into twelve equal semitones, matching a keyboard or tuner. In Mayamalavagowla the largest differences are about 14 cents, on R₁, G₃, D₁ and N₃. Tuning changes the harmonium and the target pitches used in sing-along and Shruti practice; the tanpura recording keeps its own natural intervals. Use Equal when practising with a keyboard. The differences, swara by swara, are in Tuning: Just and Equal.
Harmonium guide
All notes (default) plays every swara on the harmonium. Off (drone and click) lets you sing on your own, with only the tanpura and the tala click, while your notes are still marked. It applies only when Sing along is on; without it the harmonium always plays. In Listen, then sing the model is always played on harmonium; for your own turn, see Harmonium during your turn below.
Harmonium notes
Connected (default) joins one note smoothly to the next. Lightly separated leaves a tiny gap before each new note, so each one is distinct. Karvai holds stay sustained and written rests stay silent. Only the harmonium sound changes; the timing, tala and notation stay the same. It is not an instruction to breathe; take breaths where the music and words need them.
Tanpura drone
On by default. The tanpura sounds through the count-in, the exercise and any recovery break, and stops with Stop. Off leaves the harmonium and click without a drone.
Drone volume
0–100%, default 50%. It balances the tanpura against the harmonium and is disabled when the tanpura is off.
Harmonium volume
0–100%, default 75%. It sets the guide notes, the model in Listen, then sing, and any harmonium during your turn. It does not change the tanpura. It is applied when you release the slider.

Tala, count-in and practice

Tala click
Off by default. A click on every tala count: clap, finger count and wave each have their own sound, and in a two-kalai varnam the second count of each beat is softer. The count-in clicks even when this is off.
Count in one avarta
On by default. One full avarta of tala clicks before the exercise (sixteen counts for a two-kalai varnam), even when Tala click is off. Sing along always counts in: between the clicks the app listens to the room and to its own sound, so stay quiet until the harmonium starts. In Listen, then sing the model starts straight away; your turn counts in if this is on, and always when your notes are being marked.
Practice mode
Normal playback (default) plays the piece, or the section you chose. Listen, then sing first plays it on the harmonium for you to listen to, then gives you a turn to sing the same part. The harmonium model is never marked. Your turn is marked only when Sing along is on; without it, your turn is practice without marks and the app doesn’t use the microphone. With Repeat on, the listen-and-sing pair repeats.
Harmonium during your turn
Off by default. In Listen, then sing, your turn has only the tanpura and the click; switch this on to have the harmonium play along with you. It has no effect in normal playback. On iPhone and iPad it is the Harmonium on your turn switch, on the Response guide page.
Repeat
Off by default: the piece plays once, or in Listen, then sing, one listen-and-sing pair. On: it plays again and again until you tap Stop. It repeats what you are practising, the whole piece or the Section chosen above the notation, and never moves on to the next exercise. The section choice resets when you leave the player. Choosing a break does not turn Repeat on.
Between repeats
What happens between one repetition and the next, and in Listen, then sing also between the model and your turn. Continuous (default): no pause. Timed break: a rest of 1–30 seconds (5 at first). When ready: a rest until you tap Continue. The rest begins when the last harmonium note fades. During it the tanpura keeps playing (if on), the clicks and the moving highlight stop, and nothing you sing is marked; the count-in, if any, comes after it. In a break, Continue starts at once, Wait for me stops a timed break from ending by itself, and Stop ends practice and the tanpura. The length is for your comfort, not a breathing exercise. On iPhone and iPad this choice is greyed out in normal playback until Repeat is on.

Sing along and feedback

The microphone button in the player toolbar turns Sing along on and off. It is not in the settings sheet, but it changes how several settings behave. The first time, allow microphone access. Turn it on to have each note marked and to get a summary after each pass; see Sing along.

  • Sing along forces the count-in on when it is marking your notes, even when Count in one avarta is off. Stay quiet until the harmonium starts so the app can measure the room and its own sound.
  • In normal playback, with Sing along on and Harmonium guide off, the harmonium is silent and only the tanpura and click accompany you; the marks still appear. With Sing along off, normal playback keeps the harmonium.
  • In Listen, then sing, only your turn is marked, never the harmonium model.
  • Without Sing along, the app doesn’t use the microphone, and your turn in Listen, then sing is not marked.
  • On iPhone and iPad, the microphone indicator can stay on during breaks and while the model plays; the app ignores that sound, and nothing is analysed, stored or sent. Stop turns the microphone off. On Android the microphone pauses during those times.
Echo cancellation
On by default. When the app listens through the built-in speaker, it removes its own sound from what the microphone hears, so the harmonium is not mistaken for your voice. On iPhone and iPad the system may lower the playback volume while it is on; on any device it can slightly colour quiet singing. Turn it off when using a USB microphone away from the speaker, or headphones, and you want steady volume; with the built-in speaker, keep it on. The count-in still measures how much app sound reaches the microphone. It applies to sing-along and Shruti practice. See Microphones and headphones.

Strictness

How close a sung note must be to count, for sing-along and Shruti practice. Precise is the default. The pitch you aim for stays the same; Strictness sets how far from it a note may be and still count as in tune or close. Notes outside close are marked high or low (red); an octave error or a different swara is marked separately.

LevelIn tuneClose
Gentlewithin ±20 centswithin ±40 cents
Steadywithin ±15 centswithin ±30 cents
Precise (default)within ±10 centswithin ±25 cents

A cent is a hundredth of a semitone. Most listeners notice a difference of about 10 to 20 cents. For pieces sung with gamakas, such as gitams and varnams, Gentle is a good choice.

Gitam notation

Gitam notation chooses what a piece with words shows under each beat: Swaras, Words, or Both (the default). It applies to every worded piece — gitams, swarajatis and varnams — not only gitams. Exercises without words always show their swaras. A dash still means the previous syllable is held. It changes the display only, not playback, scoring or timing; learn the swaras first, then sing the words.

Changing settings while you practise

Settings are saved as you change them and apply to every exercise. While an exercise is playing, a change restarts it from the beginning so the new setting applies from the first note. During a break, volume, click and break changes wait for the next attempt; some other changes pause practice until you tap Continue. Changing Sa during a break stops practice.

Stored on your device

Settings are stored only on this device. There is no account and no sync. They are kept between launches; uninstalling the app removes them and your practice history. See Privacy.
