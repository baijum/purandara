Practice settings

Open the settings with the sliders button in the player. The same controls exist on Android, iPhone and iPad, though the app groups them differently. Every change is saved on the device and applies to every exercise. Tempo and Speed are on the player itself, beside the notation.

Sa and fine tune

Sa is the home note: the harmonium, the tanpura and the marking of your notes all use it. Purandara offers G2 to C4; the default is C3 (1 kattai). The list is grouped by the voices that usually sing there, from lower voices to higher voices, and each choice shows its keyboard note and kattai, such as “D3 · 2 kattai”. You can also choose Sa from the home screen. More in Choose your Sa.

Fine tune moves Sa by up to 50 cents above or below the keyboard note, in one-cent steps; exact (0) is the default. A fine-tuned Sa is shown after the label, such as “C3 · 1 kattai +12¢”. It applies to the harmonium, the tanpura and the marking of your notes.

If a piece needs notes above the harmonium samples at the chosen Sa, the player says so and offers the nearest Sa that fits. You cannot start the piece until you choose a playable Sa.

On Android, Fine tune is directly below Sa; on iPhone and iPad it is at the top of the Sa list.

Tempo and Speed

Tempo is the speed of the tala beat: 30 to 120 beats per minute, in steps of 5, default 60. It is not the speed of the written notes.

Speed is the kala: 1 to 4, default 1. Each step doubles the number of written notation cells played in each tala beat. A varisai written one cell to a beat has one cell per beat at first kala, two at second, four at third and eight at fourth. A piece already written several cells to a beat starts higher: a swarajati writes four to a beat, so at second kala it has eight. The tala beat keeps the tempo above.

At a faster speed a short piece may be played more than once to fill whole tala cycles; the line under the notation then says “Pass 2 of 4”. A varnam section already marked at double speed plays twice as fast again.

Sound and accompaniment

Tuning
Just (tanpura) is the default: each swara takes its natural ratio to Sa, the way a tanpura is tuned. Equal (keyboard) divides the octave into twelve equal semitones, matching a keyboard or tuner. In Mayamalavagowla the largest differences are about 14 cents, on R₁, G₃, D₁ and N₃. Tuning changes the harmonium and the target pitches used in sing-along and Shruti practice; the tanpura recording keeps its own natural intervals. Use Equal when practising with a keyboard.
Harmonium guide
All notes (default) plays every swara on the harmonium. In normal playback, with Sing along on, Off (drone and click) silences the harmonium so only the tanpura and tala click accompany you, while the marks still appear; with Sing along off, the harmonium stays on. In Listen, then sing the model always uses the harmonium; Harmonium during your turn controls your turn.
Harmonium notes
Connected (default) joins successive notes smoothly, with a short crossfade. Lightly separated fades the previous unheld note a little early, so each new note is distinct. Karvai holds stay sustained and written rests stay silent. It changes only the harmonium sound: note starts, tala, cursor, tempo, notation and the length of the exercise are unchanged. It is not an instruction to breathe; take breaths where the music and words need them.
Tanpura drone
On by default. The tanpura sounds through the count-in, the exercise and any recovery break, and stops with Stop. Off leaves the harmonium and click without a drone.
Drone volume
0–100%, default 50%. It balances the tanpura against the harmonium and is disabled when the tanpura is off.
Harmonium volume
0–100%, default 75%. It sets the guide notes, the model in Listen, then sing, and any harmonium during your turn. It does not change the tanpura. It is applied when you release the slider.

Tala, count-in and practice

Tala click
Off by default. A click on every tala count: clap, finger count and wave each have their own sound, and in a two-kalai varnam the second count of each beat is softer. The count-in clicks even when this is off.
Count in one avarta
On by default. One full avarta of tala clicks before the exercise — sixteen counts for a two-kalai varnam. It is separate from Tala click and sounds even when clicks are off. Sing along switches it on automatically: the app uses the gaps between count-in clicks to measure the room and its own sound, so stay quiet until the harmonium starts. In Listen, then sing the model starts without a count-in; your turn follows the count-in setting, and always counts in when it is being scored.
Practice mode
Normal playback (default) plays the exercise or the selected section or practice unit. Listen, then sing plays a harmonium model, applies the Between repeats choice, counts in if Count in one avarta is on (or if the turn is being scored), and gives you a turn at the same unit. With Repeat off there is one model and one turn; with it on the pair repeats. The model is always harmonium and is never scored. Your turn is scored only when Sing along is on and the microphone is available. Without Sing along, it is an unscored practice interval and the app does not ask for the microphone.
Harmonium during your turn
Off by default. In Listen, then sing, the model always uses the harmonium, even when Harmonium guide is off. Your turn uses the tanpura and the click; switch this on to add harmonium support. It has no effect in normal playback. On iPhone and iPad the page is called Response guide and the switch Harmonium on your turn.
Repeat
Off by default. Off plays one normal attempt, or one listen–sing pair. On repeats the current unit — the whole piece, or the selected Section or reviewed practice phrase when one is available — until you tap Stop; it does not move on to the next exercise. Choose the unit above the notation; it resets when you leave the player. In Listen, then sing it repeats the model-and-turn pair. Choosing a break does not turn Repeat on.
Between repeats
Only relevant with Repeat on, except in Listen, then sing, where it also separates the model from your turn. Continuous (default) has no extra wait: normal playback loops seamlessly, and Listen, then sing follows the release with the next part. Timed break rests for 1–30 seconds, initially 5. When ready waits for Continue with no deadline. A break starts after the harmonium release; the tanpura continues if on; the exercise clicks, cursor and tala highlight stop; nothing sung during it is marked. The next count-in is additional to the break. Continue starts at once, Wait for me cancels a timed restart, and Stop ends practice and the drone. The seconds are a convenience, not a breathing target. On iPhone and iPad the choice is disabled when Repeat is off in normal playback.

Sing along and feedback

The microphone button in the player toolbar turns Sing along on and off. It is not in the settings sheet, but it changes how several settings behave. The first time, allow microphone access. Turn it on to have each note marked and to get a summary after each pass; see Sing along.

  • Sing along forces the count-in on when it is marking your notes, even when Count in one avarta is off. Stay quiet until the harmonium starts so the app can measure the room and its own sound.
  • In normal playback, with Sing along on and Harmonium guide off, the harmonium is silent and only the tanpura and click accompany you; the marks still appear. With Sing along off, normal playback keeps the harmonium.
  • In Listen, then sing, only your turn is scored. The model is never scored, even with Sing along on.
  • Without Sing along, no microphone is requested for normal playback, and the turn in Listen, then sing is unscored.
  • On iPhone and iPad with pitch feedback on, the microphone connection and its system indicator can stay on during breaks and model playback; those buffers are discarded before analysis and nothing is stored or sent. Stop ends the session. On Android capture stops for those intervals.
Echo cancellation
On by default. When the app listens through the built-in speaker, it removes its own sound from the microphone signal so the harmonium is not mistaken for your voice. On iPhone and iPad the system may lower the playback volume while it is on; on any device it can slightly colour quiet singing. Turn it off when using a USB microphone away from the speaker, or headphones, and you want steady volume; with the built-in speaker, keep it on. The count-in still measures how much app sound reaches the microphone. It applies to sing-along and Shruti practice. See Microphones and headphones.

Strictness

How close a sung note must be to count, for sing-along and Shruti practice. Precise is the default. The target pitch does not change; Strictness sets the width of the in tune and close zones. Notes outside close are marked high or low (red); an octave error or a different swara is marked separately.

LevelIn tuneClose
Gentlewithin ±20 centswithin ±40 cents
Steadywithin ±15 centswithin ±30 cents
Precise (default)within ±10 centswithin ±25 cents

A cent is a hundredth of a semitone. Most listeners notice a difference of about 10 to 20 cents. For pieces sung with gamakas, such as gitams and varnams, Gentle is a good choice.

Gitam notation

Gitam notation chooses what a piece with words shows under each beat: Swaras, Words, or Both (the default). It applies to every worded piece — gitams, swarajatis and varnams — not only gitams. Exercises without words always show their swaras. A dash still means the previous syllable is held. It changes the display only, not playback, scoring or timing; learn the swaras first, then sing the words.

Changing settings while you practise

Settings are saved as you change them and apply to every exercise. While an exercise is playing, a change restarts it from the beginning so the new setting applies from the first note. During a recovery break, volume, click and recovery changes can apply to the next attempt without starting it early; a change that needs a new schedule, microphone setup or drone may pause practice until you press Continue. Changing Sa during a break ends the session.

Stored on your device

Settings are stored only on this device. There is no account and no sync. They are kept between launches; uninstalling the app removes them and your practice history. See Privacy.
