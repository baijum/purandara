Practice settings

Open them with the sliders button in the player. They are remembered, and apply to every exercise. On iOS, use the named Sound, Rhythm, and Feedback and notation pages to reach the controls.

Sa and fine tune
Your home note and a nudge of up to ±50 cents. See Choose your Sa.
Tuning
Just (tanpura), the default, tunes each swara to its natural ratio with Sa, as a tanpura does. Equal (keyboard) matches a keyboard; R₁, G₃, D₁ and N₃ differ by up to about 14 cents.
Harmonium guide
All notes plays every swara. Off (drone and click) leaves only the tanpura and tala, so you sing alone.
Harmonium notes
Connected, the default, joins successive notes smoothly. Lightly separated adds a small gap to help distinguish notes; holds stay sustained. This changes the harmonium guide, not tala timing or where you breathe.
Tanpura drone, drone volume and harmonium volume
Turn the tanpura on or off, and balance it against the harmonium.
Tala click
A click on every beat, accented on the claps.
Count in one avarta
One cycle of tala clicks before the exercise starts. Sing-along always counts in.
Practice mode
Normal playback is the default. Listen, then sing plays a harmonium model, gives you a recovery interval, then a turn to sing the same exercise or selected unit. The Sing along switch controls pitch feedback; without it, no microphone access is needed.
Harmonium during your turn
In listen–repeat practice, the model always uses the harmonium at your configured volume. Your turn uses the tanpura and configured click by default; turn this option on for harmonium support.
Repeat
Off plays one normal attempt, or one listen–sing pair. On repeats the selected unit until you tap Stop; choosing a break does not turn Repeat on.
Between repeats
Continuous preserves the existing seamless normal repeat. Timed break offers 1–30 seconds, initially 5; When ready waits for Continue. In listen–repeat, this choice also separates the model and your turn. Break seconds are a convenience setting, not a breathing target.
Echo cancellation
On the speaker, removes the app’s own sound from what the microphone hears, so it isn’t mistaken for your voice. On by default. Turn it off when using a microphone placed away from the speaker. See Microphones and headphones.

Strictness

How close a note must be to count, for sing-along and Shruti practice.

LevelIn tuneClose
Gentlewithin ±20 centswithin ±40 cents
Steadywithin ±15 centswithin ±30 cents
Precise (default)within ±10 centswithin ±25 cents

A cent is a hundredth of a semitone. Most listeners notice a difference of about 10 to 20 cents. For gitams, which are sung with gamakas, Gentle is a good choice.

Gitam notation

What a gitam shows under each beat: Swaras, Words, or Both (the default). Exercises without words always show their swaras.
