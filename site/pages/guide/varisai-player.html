Play a varisai

Open any exercise from the home screen to play it on the harmonium, over the tanpura, in Adi tala.

Reading the screen

The tala strip
The eight beats of Adi tala: x clap, 1 2 3 finger counts, x clap, v wave, x clap, v wave. The beat being heard is lit.
The notation
One row per avarta (tala cycle). A single bar ends a group of the tala, a double bar ends the avarta. A comma holds the swara before it for one more beat: keep singing through it. S′ is the upper Sa. The swara being heard is highlighted.
The line below the notation
While playing, it shows your Sa, “Count-in…” during the count-in, and “Pass 2 of 4” when an exercise repeats to fill whole cycles at a faster speed.

Controls

Play and Stop
Play starts from the beginning, with one cycle of tala clicks first if count-in is on. Stop fades out.
Tempo
Beats per minute of the tala, in steps of 5. Start slow: 50 to 60 is plenty for a new exercise.
Speed
Kala 1 to 4, at the same tala tempo: each step doubles the speed of the written notation. At first kala a varisai has one cell per beat, at second two, at third four and at fourth eight. A piece already written several cells to a beat (a swarajati writes four) doubles from there. A short piece repeats to fill the tala cycle; the line below the notation says “Pass 2 of 4”.
Microphone
Turns sing-along on or off.
Practice settings
The sliders button: Sa and fine tune, tuning, harmonium guide and note separation, drone and volumes, strictness, notation, echo cancellation, click, count-in, practice mode, repeat and recovery. See Practice settings.

Changing a setting while an exercise plays restarts it with the new setting. During a recovery break, mix and recovery choices apply to the next attempt without starting it early. Changes that require a new schedule, capture setup or drone may pause practice until you press Continue; changing Sa ends the session.

Recovery between attempts

With Repeat on, choose Timed break or When ready in practice settings to rest between completed attempts. The tanpura continues if enabled; the exercise clicks, notation cursor and tala highlight stop. The break starts after the harmonium release, and the next count-in is additional to the break duration.

During a break, Continue proceeds when you are ready. Wait for me cancels a timed restart, and Stop ends practice and the drone. A break interrupted by backgrounding requires an explicit Continue on return rather than an automatic restart. Changing Sa ends the session.

One attempt includes any internal repetitions the scheduler uses to fill complete tala cycles. Neither a notation line nor a tala ending automatically becomes a breath point.

Listen, then sing

Choose this practice mode to hear a model before your turn. The model plays on harmonium; then you rest, count in and sing the same unit. With Repeat off, one model and one response play; with Repeat on, that pair repeats. Turn Sing along on if you want pitch feedback, or leave it off to practise without the microphone.

Reviewed phrases

A phrase choice appears only for units with recorded musical review. No phrase boundaries are currently released: use a whole short exercise or an existing section instead. Sections are rehearsal units, not automatically approved places to breathe. Holds and sahitya words must not be split to invent shorter phrases.

If the output changes

On both platforms, changing headphones or speakers stops playback, the tanpura and sing-along listening, with no automatic restart. Tap Play when you are ready; the next start uses the output selected in the system.

The app stops playing when it leaves the screen, for example when you switch apps or lock the phone. An output change while backgrounded also cancels any normal fresh-session resume on return.
