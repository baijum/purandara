Play a varisai

Open any exercise from the home screen to play it on the harmonium, over the tanpura, in Adi tala.

Reading the screen

The tala strip
The eight beats of Adi tala: x clap, 1 2 3 finger counts, x clap, v wave, x clap, v wave. The beat being heard is lit: keep tala with it. Each mark stands over the swaras sung on that beat.
The notation
One block per avarta (tala cycle). A single bar ends a group of the tala, a double bar ends the avarta. A comma holds the swara before it for one more place: keep singing through it. S′ is the upper Sa. The swara being heard is highlighted: sing what this highlight shows.
The line below the notation
While playing, it shows your Sa, “Count-in…” during the count-in, and “Pass 2 of 4” when an exercise repeats to fill whole cycles at a faster speed.

At a faster speed

In Carnatic practice the hand keeps the same beat in every speed, and more swaras fit into each beat. The player lays the notation out the same way: each block is still one tala cycle, and the swaras that share a beat sit together on a light band. The bars move with them, so they still fall where the hand claps or waves.

When a cycle takes more than two rows, the strip stays on one row and each row of notation starts with the mark of its first beat (x, 1, v) in the margin. Where a short piece fills less than a cycle, the next pass carries on in the same block: in third speed the plain scale is written twice in one cycle, as you would sing it. The tempo still sets how fast the beats go.

Controls

Play and Stop
Play starts from the beginning, with one cycle of tala clicks first if count-in is on. Stop fades out.
Tempo
Beats per minute of the tala, in steps of 5. Start slow: 50 to 60 is plenty for a new exercise.
Speed
Kala 1 to 4, at the same tala tempo: each step doubles the number of swaras to a beat. In first kala a varisai has one swara per beat, in second two, in third four and in fourth eight. A piece written with several swaras to a beat (a swarajati has four) doubles from there. The notation is regrouped by beat; see At a faster speed. A short piece repeats to fill the tala cycle; the line below the notation says “Pass 2 of 4”.
Microphone
Turns sing-along on or off.
Practice settings
The sliders button: Sa and fine tune, tuning, harmonium guide and note separation, drone and volumes, strictness, notation, echo cancellation, click, count-in, practice mode, repeat and recovery. See Practice settings.

Changing a setting while an exercise plays restarts it with the new setting. During a break, volume and break changes wait for the next attempt; some other changes pause practice until you tap Continue, and changing Sa stops it.

Resting between attempts

With Repeat on, choose Timed break or When ready under Between repeats in practice settings to rest between attempts. The tanpura keeps playing (if on); the clicks and the moving highlight stop. The rest begins when the last harmonium note fades, and the count-in comes after it.

During a break, Continue starts the next attempt when you are ready. Wait for me cancels a timed restart, and Stop ends practice and the drone. If you leave the app during a break, tap Continue when you come back. Changing Sa stops practice.

An attempt is the whole exercise, including any passes needed to fill complete tala cycles. The end of a line or a tala cycle is not a breathing point in itself; breathe where the music and the words allow.

Listen, then sing

Choose this practice mode to hear the piece before you sing it. First the harmonium plays the piece or section for you to listen to; then, after any break and the count-in, you sing the same part. With Repeat off this happens once; with Repeat on, the pair repeats. Turn Sing along on if you want pitch feedback, or leave it off to practise without the microphone.

Practising phrase by phrase

Practice by phrase will appear only for phrases a musician has checked, and none are available yet. For now, practise a whole short exercise or one section. A section is a part to rehearse, not a place to breathe.

If you change headphones or speakers

Changing headphones or speakers stops playback, the tanpura and sing-along. Nothing restarts by itself: tap Play when you are ready, and the sound comes from whatever your device now has selected.

The app stops playing when it leaves the screen, for example when you switch apps or lock the phone.
