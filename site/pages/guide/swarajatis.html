Swarajatis

After the gitams come the swarajatis: longer songs, written four swaras to a beat. Purandara has Rara Venu Gopa Bala in raga Bilahari, Adi tala.

Four swaras to a beat

Each beat of the tala holds four notation cells, so one cycle of Adi tala is 32 cells. The player shows each cycle on four rows of eight cells (two beats a row). A comma holds the swara before it through that cell.

At the default tempo of 60 that is four swaras a second. Lower the tempo to learn a line, then bring it back up.

Swaras first, then words

Each charanam is sung twice: first as swaras (the headings say Charanam 1 · swaram), then to the same tune with its words (Charanam 1 · sahityam). After each charanam the pallavi returns.

The Gitam notation setting (Swaras, Words or Both) applies to swarajatis too.

Bilahari

Bilahari climbs S R₂ G₃ P D₂ S′ and comes down S′ N₃ D₂ P M₁ G₃ R₂ S. Rara Venu goes down to lower Dha. Like the gitams, it is sung with gamakas the harmonium doesn’t play; Gentle strictness suits sing-along here.
