Swarajatis

After the gitams come the swarajatis: longer songs, written several swaras to a beat. Purandara has Rara Venu Gopa Bala in raga Bilahari, and Sambasiva Yenave in raga Khamas, both in Adi tala.

Several swaras to a beat

Rara Venu writes four notation cells to each beat, so one cycle of Adi tala is 32 cells; the player shows each cycle on four rows of eight cells (two beats a row). Sambasiva writes two cells to a beat — a cycle is 16 cells, on two rows — and its last charanam is in khanda gati, five cells to a beat (a cycle of 40 cells, a row per beat). The tala beat itself stays the same; only the subdivision changes. A comma holds the swara before it through that cell.

At the default tempo of 60 a beat takes a second. Lower the tempo to learn a line, then bring it back up.

Swaras first, then words

Each charanam is sung twice: first as swaras (the headings say Charanam 1 · swaram), then to the same tune with its words (Charanam 1 · sahityam). After each charanam the pallavi returns. Sambasiva has its own anupallavi, sung like the pallavi.

To work on one charanam, choose it with Section above the notation (see Gitams).

The Gitam notation setting (Swaras, Words or Both) applies to swarajatis too.

Bilahari

Bilahari climbs S R₂ G₃ P D₂ S′ and comes down S′ N₃ D₂ P M₁ G₃ R₂ S. Rara Venu goes down to lower Dha. Like the gitams, it is sung with gamakas the harmonium doesn’t play; Gentle strictness suits sing-along here.

Khamas

Khamas climbs S M₁ G₃ M₁ P D₂ N₂ S′ — no Ri on the way up, Ma and Ga in pairs — and comes down S′ N₂ D₂ P M₁ G₃ R₂ S. Sambasiva stays in one octave. It is sung with gamakas too; Gentle strictness suits sing-along here.

Rara Venu’s notation follows karnatik.com. Sambasiva’s follows Sangeeta Bala Padam, Part 1 (pp. 82–84). Both are checked cell by cell against Shivkumar Kalyanaraman’s archive; see Credits.
