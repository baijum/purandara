Recordings

Sing anything, a varisai, a gitam line, a phrase you are unsure of, and see which swaras the app heard. Nothing is shown while you sing; the swaras appear when you stop.

Make a recording

  1. On the home screen, in the Your Sa (Shruthi) card, tap Recordings, then New recording.
  2. The screen shows the Sa the swaras will be read against: your current Sa. Set your Sa first if it is wrong, or change it afterwards.
  3. Tap Start recording, sing, then tap Stop recording. A recording can be up to 10 minutes long.
  4. The app finds the swaras (a few seconds) and opens the recording.

No tanpura plays while you record: it would be heard in the recording and confuse the swaras. If you want a drone, play your own tanpura or shruti box and set your Sa in the app to match it. Headphones aren’t needed.

If you leave the app, take a call or the screen locks while recording, the recording stops and keeps what it has.

The swaras heard

The swaras are shown a line per phrase: a pause of a second and a half or more starts a new line, so a breath between notes keeps them on one line. A comma after a swara means it was held, one for each extra length, as in the app’s notation. Swaras outside the raga you chose are shown in red.

The reading is approximate. The app looks for the places where your voice stays steady on one swara for at least a tenth of a second:

  • A slide (jaru) between two swaras shows only the two swaras.
  • A gamaka is read as the note it rests on. An oscillation that never settles may show no swara at all.
  • Very fast phrases can lose notes.

So always check the swaras against your pitch, drawn below them.

Your pitch

Below the swaras, your voice is drawn as dots against lines for the raga’s swaras, with each swara the app read drawn as a bar. The view shows about eight seconds and follows playback; tap it to play from that point. With reduced motion turned on, it moves a page at a time instead of scrolling.

Play it back

Tap Play. The swara being sung is highlighted, and the cursor moves along your pitch. Drag the position slider to move, or tap a phrase to play from its start.

Sa and raga

Changing Sa (and its fine tune) or Raga on a recording reads the swaras again at once; nothing is recorded again.

  • Sa: if you sang at a different pitch from your usual Sa, choose the Sa you actually sang to.
  • Raga: with None, every swara gets its default name (R₂, G₃, D₂, N₃ for the shared positions). Choosing a raga names the swaras as that raga does and marks any that it doesn’t use. The ragas offered are the app’s ragas.

The swaras use the tuning in your practice settings (Just or Equal).

Titles, renaming and deleting

A recording is named automatically from its raga, Sa and time, for example “Mayamalavagowla · Sa D · 15 Nov, 6:42 pm”; choosing a raga later updates the name. Tap Rename to give it your own title; leave the title empty to go back to the automatic one. Delete asks first, and can’t be undone. In the list you can also swipe a recording to delete it (iPhone and iPad).

Where recordings are kept

Recordings are saved only on your device, inside the app. They are never sent anywhere, and the app has no sharing or export. Deleting the app deletes them. See Privacy.
