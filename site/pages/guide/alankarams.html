Alankarams

After the varisais come the seven alankarams. Each is one climbing figure, sung up to upper Sa and back down, in a different tala. This is where you first keep talas other than Adi.

The seven talas

Each tala is built from three kinds of anga: a laghu (a clap, then finger counts), a drutam (a clap and a wave) and an anudrutam (a single clap). The length of a laghu is its jati.

The player shows the tala under the title (“Mayamalavagowla · Dhruva tala”). Turn on the tala click to hear it: the clap, the finger counts and the wave each have their own sound, so you can follow a long laghu by ear.

Long talas wrap onto two rows

A tala longer than eight beats is shown on two rows, split where an anga starts: Dhruva as 4+2 and 4+4, Matya as 4+2 and 4, Jhampa as 7 and 1+2, Ata as 5 and 5+2+2. The tala strip wraps the same way, so each swara stays under its beat. Screen readers still read each avarta as one sentence.

Practising them

  • Clap the tala along with the click before you sing, then sing with it.
  • Sing each alankaram in the first speed, then in the second and third (Speed 2 and 3).
  • Sing-along, count-in, repeat and every other setting work as they do for the varisais.
