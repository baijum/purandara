Getting started

Purandara plays the graded Carnatic lessons in tala at your Sa, and listens while you sing. This guide walks through everything it does.

Your first practice

  1. Set your Sa. On the home screen, the Your Sa (Shruthi) card shows the current Sa, such as “C3 · 1 kattai”. Tap it to choose another. If you don’t know your Sa, use Find my Sa.
  2. Warm up. Tap Play tanpura and hum Sa until it feels settled, or open Practise Sa to see your pitch as you sing.
  3. Play an exercise. Tap Start here to open the plain scale, then tap Play. Listen to one cycle before you sing.
  4. Sing along. Tap the microphone button at the top of the player to have your notes marked. See Sing along.

Your Sa, a tanpura to tune to, and the way into Shruti practice and Find my Sa.
The sets
Every exercise, grouped by set in teaching order. Each set says what it trains, and once you have practised some, how many. Each exercise shows a small line of its melodic shape, a tick once practised, and your best sing-along result.

An exercise counts as practised once you have heard one whole pass of it to the end. Opening it, or stopping during the count-in, doesn’t count.

What’s in the app

50 pieces. The varisais and alankarams are in raga Mayamalavagowla; the varisais in Adi tala, each alankaram in its own tala. The gitams are in raga Malahari.

SetExercisesWhat it trains
Plain scale1The seven swaras up and down, settled against the tanpura.
Sarali Varisai16A short figure set against the plain ascent and descent: steady pitch, even timing.
Madhya Sthayi Varisai5Longer figures across the octave; later, teacher-guided studies.
Janta Varisai7Each swara sung as a pair: clean, even repeated notes.
Vakra Janta Varisai2Paired notes in zigzags: finding the next note without sliding.
Dhattu Varisai6Leaps over a note and back: reaching a distant pitch without sliding.
Alankaram7One climbing figure in each of the seven suladi talas (Dhruva, Matya, Rupaka, Jhampa, Triputa, Ata, Eka): keeping the tala as you sing. Talas longer than eight beats wrap onto two rows. More
Gitam5Purandara Dasa’s first songs in raga Malahari, then Analekara in Suddha Saveri, with their words under the swaras. More
Swarajati1Rara Venu Gopa Bala (Bilahari): four swaras to a beat, each charanam sung as swaras and then with words. More

Purandara is a practice companion, not a teacher. Gamakas, the traditional paired-note emphasis in Janta varisai, and the octave range for the later studies are best learned from a teacher.
