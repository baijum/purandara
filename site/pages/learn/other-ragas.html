The varisais in other ragas

Once the varisais are settled in Mayamalavagowla, the NIOS textbooks suggest singing the same exercises in other ragas, such as Shankarabharanam, Kharaharapriya or Kalyani, so the ear learns new swaras on patterns the voice already knows. This page shows the six melakartas Purandara can play the varisais in.

What changes, and what doesn’t

Each of these ragas is a melakarta: all seven swaras, one form of each, the same up and down, just like Mayamalavagowla. So every varisai keeps its letters and its shape; only some swaras move. Sa and Pa never move. Ri, Ga, Ma, Dha and Ni each have their forms (see The seven swaras), and each raga picks one.

A good way in: sing the plain scale in the new raga slowly against the drone, listening for the swaras that moved, then the first Sarali varisais. The table compares the seven.

Natabhairavi (melakarta 20)

Up: S R₂ G₂ M₁ P D₁ N₂ S′ · Down: S′ N₂ D₁ P M₁ G₂ R₂ S

Compared with Mayamalavagowla: Ri a key higher (R₁→R₂); Ga a key lower (G₃→G₂); Ni a key lower (N₃→N₂). On a keyboard, with Sa on C, these are the same keys as the natural minor scale (Aeolian).

Against the drone

The keyboard comparisons above are for finding the keys. Sung against a tanpura, the swaras settle into their own places, a little off the keyboard’s keys, as in Mayamalavagowla. Tuning: Just and Equal has the figures for every swara position.

Which ragas to take the varisais into, and in what order, is your teacher’s choice; these six are the ones Purandara offers, not a rule.

Practise this in Purandara

  • The varisais in another raga: choose the raga on Home, and hear each one’s scale before you choose.
  • Swara numbers: see R₂ G₂ in the notation while you sing.
  • Sing along: check that the swaras that moved land in their new places.
