Words used here

The Carnatic words on these pages, in one place, each with a link to where it is explained. Many have more than one spelling; the commonest alternatives are in brackets.

Akshara (aksharakala)
One beat of the tala. Adi tala
Alankaram
An exercise of one climbing figure, set in one of the seven basic talas; the seven alankarams come after the varisais. Alankarams
Anga
A part of a tala: laghu, drutam or anudrutam. Adi tala
Anudrutam
An anga of one beat: a single beat of the hand. Adi tala
Arohanam
A raga’s scale going up. The seven swaras
Avarohanam
A raga’s scale coming down. The seven swaras
Avarta (avartanam)
One complete cycle of the tala. Adi tala
Dhattu varisai
Varisais that leap over a swara and back. The varisai sets
Drone
A sound that keeps Sa (and usually Pa) going under your singing, such as the tanpura. Shruti and Sa
Drutam
An anga of two beats: a beat, then a wave. Adi tala
Gamaka
A movement or oscillation around a swara that gives a raga its character. Mayamalavagowla
Gitam
The first songs a student learns, with words, after the exercises. Gitams
Janta varisai
Varisais with every swara sung twice, in pairs. The varisai sets
Janya raga
A raga taken from a melakarta: it may leave out swaras or take them in a crooked order.
Jati
The length of a laghu: 3, 4, 5, 7 or 9 beats. Adi tala
Kala
A speed: how many swaras fit into each beat of the tala. The speeds
Kalai
Counting more than one count to each beat of the hand, as in Adi tala in two kalai. Adi tala
Karvai
A held note, written with a comma: the swara before it lasts one more beat. The varisai sets
Kattai
A way of naming your Sa by harmonium key: 1 is C, 5 is G. Shruti and Sa
Laghu
An anga of a beat followed by finger counts; four beats long in Adi tala. Adi tala
Madhya sthayi
The middle octave, from your Sa up to Ni. The seven swaras
Mandra sthayi
The octave below your Sa. The seven swaras
Melakarta
A parent scale: all seven swaras, one form of each, the same up and down. There are 72; Mayamalavagowla is number 15. Mayamalavagowla
Raga
A melodic framework: a set of swaras, the way it moves up and down, and the phrases and gamakas that make it recognisable.
Sa (shadjam)
The home note, chosen to suit your voice; every other swara is measured from it. Shruti and Sa
Sarali varisai
The first exercises: short figures against the plain scale. The varisai sets
Shruti (sruti)
Your pitch, the note you have chosen as Sa; in theory, also the fine steps of pitch smaller than a swara. Shruti and Sa
Sthayi
An octave: mandra (lower), madhya (middle) or tara (upper). The seven swaras
Swara
A note: Sa, Ri, Ga, Ma, Pa, Dha or Ni. The seven swaras
Swarajati
A practice song with words and swara passages, learned before the varnam. Swarajatis
Swarasthana
One of the twelve places a swara can sit in the octave. The seven swaras
Tala
A repeating cycle of beats, kept with the hand. Adi tala
Tanpura (tambura)
A long-necked string instrument that plays the drone. Shruti and Sa
Tara sthayi
The upper octave, from upper Sa upwards. The seven swaras
Vakra
Crooked: swaras taken out of their straight order up or down. The varisai sets
Varisai
A graded practice exercise; the sets are Sarali, Sthayi, Janta and Dhattu. The varisai sets
Varnam
A longer piece that links the practice lessons and concert music, sung in two speeds. Varnams
